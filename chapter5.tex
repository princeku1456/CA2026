\subsection{Science Collaboration as Space Compass: NASA's Lunar South Pole Mission \& Geopolitics of Space}

\subsubsection*{NASA Lunar Roadmap -- Three Phases}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{2026--2029}: Surface missions; scientific experiments.
  \item \textbf{Late 2020s--early 2030s}: ``Initial operations''; potential \textbf{nuclear power installations}.
  \item \textbf{Post-2032}: \textbf{Semi-permanent human settlement} on lunar South Pole.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Why Lunar South Pole?}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Shorter shadow periods → consistent \textbf{solar energy generation} (unlike 2-week light/dark cycles elsewhere on Moon).
  \item \textbf{Permanently Shadowed Craters}: water ice deposits → rocket fuel, life support, habitability.
  \item Ice records: history of water/material movement through solar system → insights into \textbf{conditions for emergence of life}.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Moon as Gateway to Deep Space}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Staging ground for \textbf{Mars missions}.
  \item Study human physiological adaptation to \textbf{reduced gravity} before deep-space voyages.
  \item Long-duration habitation tech + resource utilisation testing.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Geopolitics of Space}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{China}: lunar rovers + sample-return missions; robust programme.
  \item \textbf{China--Russia}: \textbf{International Lunar Research Station (ILRS)} -- planned within 100 km of lunar South Pole; nuclear-powered.
  \item \textbf{USA}: \textbf{Artemis Programme} -- led by NASA; competes with ILRS.
  \item Unlike Cold War's symbolic space race → current competition = \textbf{early access to strategically significant extraterrestrial regions and resources}.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Private Sector Role}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Blue Origin} (Jeff Bezos): heavy-lift rockets, lunar landers, early infrastructure for NASA's lunar base.
  \item \textbf{SpaceX} (Elon Musk): central partner in broader US space ambitions.
  \item Reflects growing importance of \textbf{public-private partnerships} in space exploration.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Space Governance: Challenges}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Cold War-era legal framework (Outer Space Treaty, 1967) \textbf{ill-equipped} for current dimensions.
  \item Risks: competing legal regimes; fragmentation of scientific cooperation; resource/territorial disputes.
  \item \textbf{Artemis Accords} (US-led) vs. \textbf{ILRS framework} (China-Russia) → parallel governance architectures.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Way Forward}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Updated \textbf{multilateral norms} for peaceful, sustainable outer space use.
  \item \textbf{Science, not geopolitics}, must drive space exploration.
  \item International collaboration essential -- shared challenges (climate change, tech development) demand cooperative frameworks.
\end{itemize}
