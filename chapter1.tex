\subsection{Transition from MGNREGA to VB-GRAM G Act, 2025: Analysis of Draft Rules}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Structural Changes: VB-GRAM G vs. MGNREGA:}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Employment Days:} Increases guaranteed rural wage employment from 100 days to \textbf{125 days} per year.
        \item \textbf{Allocation Model:} Flips the bottom-up, demand-driven model of MGNREGA (based on state labor budgets) to a top-down \textbf{“Normative Allocation”} system determined by the Centre.
        \item \textbf{Funding Devolution Formula:} Center will use the \textbf{16th Finance Commission's} horizontal devolution recommendations to determine state-wise normative allocation.
        \item \textbf{Fiscal Burden:} Moves away from the Center paying 100\% of the wage bill under MGNREGA; shifts a significant portion of the funding burden onto states. States must bear 100\% of any expenditure incurred in excess of their normative allocation.
        \item \textbf{Agricultural Seasonal Clause:} Introduces a mandatory \textbf{60-day pause} during peak sowing and harvesting seasons to prevent rural farm labor shortages.
        \item \textbf{Performance-Linked Allocations:} A portion of the normative fund will be kept aside and distributed based on state efficiency indicators: timely wage payments, compliance with social audits, and percentage of project completion within a financial year.
    \end{enumerate}

    \item \textbf{Worker Transition Framework:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Card Validity:} Existing MGNREGA job cards remain valid for seeking employment under VB-GRAM G, provided they are renewed and verified through e-KYC. This acts as a transitional arrangement until state governments issue new \textbf{Gramin Rozgar Guarantee Cards}.
        \item \textbf{Continuity of Legal Guarantees:} Both the \textbf{Unemployment Allowance} and the \textbf{Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT)} mechanism into verified bank or post office accounts are retained.
        \item \textbf{Pending Clause:} The Union government is yet to declare the minimum wage rate under the new scheme.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Critical Analysis \& Concerns:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Dilution of Rights-Based Approach:} Shifting from a demand-driven model to a pre-fixed central normative allocation risks weakening the legal "right to work" tenet that formed the core of MGNREGA.
        \item \textbf{Implications for Fiscal Federalism:} Forcing states to bear excess expenditure beyond the top-down normative limit, combined with decreased allocations for historically proactive states, could trigger severe Centre–State fiscal friction.
        \item \textbf{Rural Wage Dynamics:} While the 60-day seasonal pause protects landowners by maintaining farm labor availability, it may restrict immediate alternative income generation for vulnerable landless laborers during transition windows.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming (PROG) Rules, 2026}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sectoral Classification \& Market Data:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Market Scale:} Indian online gaming market valued at INR 232 billion (2024), projected to grow at 11\% CAGR to INR 316 billion by 2027. Transaction-based gaming constitutes 77\% of total revenue.
        \item \textbf{Esports:} Competitive, strategy, and skill-based digital sports integrated into multi-sport events.
        \item \textbf{Online Social Games:} Casual, skill-based entertainment or learning games (classified as safe).
        \item \textbf{Online Money Games:} Platforms involving financial stakes (chance/skill mix). Impacted $\sim$45 crore people with cumulative losses exceeding Rs. 20,000 crores.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Core Pillars of the Framework:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Online Gaming Authority of India:} Digital-first attached office under MeitY, chaired by Additional Secretary (MeitY) with JS-level members from Home, Finance, I\&B, Sports, and Law.
        \item \textbf{Determination Mechanism:} Objective evaluation (stakes, monetary winnings, asset monetization) completed within 90 days via suo motu action or application to classify games.
        \item \textbf{Registration Regime:} Mandatory for e-sports and notified social games. Issues a digital Certificate of Registration valid up to 10 years. Money games are explicitly excluded from registration.
        \item \textbf{User Safety Mandates:} Technical and behavioral safeguards including age-gating, time limits, parental controls, data retention, and integrity monitoring.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Key Issues Addressed:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Social Risks:} Rising incidence of digital addiction, severe financial distress, money laundering, and related suicides.
        \item \textbf{Regulatory Ambiguity:} Overlapping definitions of chance versus skill that previously enabled illegal betting platforms to masquerade as casual games.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Enforcement and Penalties:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Prohibitions:} Complete ban on offering, advertising, or facilitating online money games. Banks/payment gateways barred from processing related transactions.
        \item \textbf{Criminal Liabilities:} First offense for operating money games attracts up to 3 years imprisonment and/or Rs. 1 crore fine. Repeat offenses carry 3--5 years imprisonment and Rs. 1--2 crore fine. Illegal advertising attracts up to 2 years imprisonment or Rs. 50 lakh fine.
        \item \textbf{Investigating Agency:} Empowered Cyber Cell officers at State/UT police station, district, and Commissionerate levels. Digital blocking executed via the IT Act, 2000.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Way Forward:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Harmonize state-level gambling laws with the federal PROG Act to avoid jurisdictional conflicts.
        \item Establish robust algorithmic filters to dynamically track and block proxy mirror sites of banned money games.
        \item Boost indigenous game development ecosystem leveraging the explicit legal safety provided to e-sports.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Supreme Court Judgement on Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of Electoral Rolls}

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Four Core Legal Issues Framed by the Court:}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Whether the ECI possesses the structural power to conduct the impugned SIR.
        \item Whether the SIR is founded on a legitimate purpose, and whether the adopted measures are proportionate to the object sought to be achieved.
        \item Whether the procedure adopted is contrary to, or in violation of, the provisions of the \textit{Representation of the People Act, 1950} and the \textit{Registration of Electors Rules, 1960}.
        \item Whether, under its constitutional mandate to prepare and maintain ERs, the ECI is empowered to scrutinize the citizenship status of individuals seeking inclusion or continuation in the rolls.
    \end{enumerate}

    \item \textbf{Key Supreme Court Observations \& Remarks:}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Intersection of Constitutional Concerns:} The SC noted that the issue rests at the intersection of two constitutional duties: including all eligible citizens, and ensuring that the ER reflects the true composition of the political community.
        \item \textbf{Institutional Deference:} The Court delivered a complete "clean chit" to the ECI, characterizing its process and methodology as \textbf{"optimal"}. It observed that it is not open to the Court to supplant its own judgment in matters concerning execution for which the ECI is endowed with specialized \textbf{"institutional expertise"}.
        \item \textbf{Systemic Nature of Errors:} The Court was convinced that cumulative inaccuracies over an extended period had become systemic in nature. It recognized that such deep-seated inaccuracies require a comprehensive SIR because they do not readily admit to piecemeal or localized solutions.
        \item \textbf{Limited Inquiry vs. Citizenship Finality:} The SC explicitly reaffirmed that while the ECI is empowered to undertake a limited inquiry into citizenship to satisfy voter eligibility, this does not equal a final legal determination of citizenship in a strict sense. It merely reflects the ECI's contextual inability to be satisfied for electoral inclusion, leaving the voter's ultimate claim to citizenship open for competent statutory adjudication.
        \item \textbf{Test of Proportionality:} The Court held that the exercise successfully met the parameters of proportionality, as it integrated sufficient procedural safeguards to prevent arbitrary or widespread disenfranchisement. Post-exercise data did not demonstrate any systemic constitutional infirmity in design.
    \end{enumerate}

    \item \textbf{Directions Issued to the Election Commission of India (ECI):}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Mandatory Reference Window:} The ECI is legally bound to disclose the exact count of "ineligible" persons discovered through the SIR and refer their names to the competent authority under the \textit{Citizenship Act, 1955} within a strict \textbf{four-week} deadline for formal citizenship adjudication.
        \item \textbf{Strict Pre-election Timeline:} This adjudication must be completed by the competent authority, following due notice and hearings, \textbf{before} the next impending Parliamentary, Legislative Assembly, or Local Body elections in the respective state or constituency.
        \item \textbf{Accountability and Public Disclosure:} Having received judicial backing, the ECI is now expected to present a complete, transparent report card comparing the post-SIR health of the electoral rolls (on parameters of integrity, accuracy, and purity) against prior rolls.
    \end{enumerate}

    \item \textbf{Critical Concerns and "Structural Correctives":}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{The Evolution of Safeguards:} The SC admitted that the SIR process, as initially designed, did raise legitimate concerns regarding documentation, transparency, and access. 
        \item \textbf{Judicial Infusion of Fairness:} The Court highlighted that its own progressive, interim interventions served as vital structural correctives. These interventions forced the ECI to adopt more inclusive standards, such as:
        \begin{enumerate}
            \item Treating the \textbf{Aadhaar card} as an additional, valid 12th document of identity verification.
            \item Mandating the public disclosure of the draft roll containing approximately \textbf{65 lakh excluded electors} in Bihar, accompanied explicitly by the individual reasons for their deletion, ensuring a verifiable and contestable administrative process.
        \end{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{The Scale of Deletions vs. Access to Justice:} Critical observers (including former election commissioners) note that while the court order delivers a massive legal victory to the ECI, massive hurdles remain for the affected populace. Hundreds of thousands of deleted individuals—such as the massive block of over \textbf{27 lakh electors excluded in West Bengal}—face immense logistical hardships while navigating the appellate process and waiting for structural justice.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}
